Same orders, different brokers

Since August 2026, US brokers publish monthly execution statistics under amended SEC Rule 605. Raw numbers mix how well a broker executes with what its customers trade. This tracker compares brokers on identical orders (same stock, lot type, order size and order type, same month) and reports how much better or worse each broker's customers did than other brokers' customers on exactly those orders.

How to read it

Gap is the adjusted difference in E/Q, the effective spread divided by the quoted spread (lower is better for the customer). A gap of −0.20 means the broker's customers paid an effective spread 20 percentage points of the quoted spread smaller than other brokers achieved on the same orders. Bars show 95% confidence intervals from a stock-level bootstrap. Rank intervals show how uncertain each position is. Raw rank is where the broker would sit on its unadjusted average, which mixes in what its customers trade. $ per $10k converts the adjusted percentage effective spread into dollars per $10,000 traded.

The method, its proofs and its limitations are in the paper. The analysis was pre-registered before any broker was compared. Within-cell differences in order timing or customer information are not removed; the paper shows they are unlikely to explain most gaps. Reports are self-reported by brokers, and some brokers' reports could not be collected.

Data: SEC Rule 605 reports published by each broker (). Code, pre-registration and paper: github.com/GruntingRhino/retail-execution-quality. Not investment advice.
